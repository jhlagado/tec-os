CAVERNS -- JOHN HARDY'S BASIC ADVENTURE, REFINED FOR TEC-OS

THE LEGEND OF SVARTALFHEIM

Deep in the icy mountains of northern Iotunheim lived a band of elves
who called themselves the Great Sons of Svartalfheim. They mined the
limestone caves for precious minerals and fashioned them into jewellery.
Merchants came from Asgar and Fenris to buy their work. The elves grew
prosperous and linked their mines to a golden city beneath the mountains.

The greedy King of the Danes sent his troops to sack the city and took
the elves away to work in his royal foundry. A strange illness swept
through them. Within weeks they were all dead and their gifts were lost.

Centuries later a goatherd named Peter came running into town. High in
the hills he had found a cave guarded by a green serpent that breathed
fire. He escaped the dragon and found a room full of gold and silver.
The only proof he brought home was a tiny ruby ring of exquisite
workmanship. When he led the villagers back beyond Mount Ymir, a rockfall
killed them before they reached the entrance. The hoard passed into myth.

Four hundred years have passed since Peter's day. You have followed the
valley into the foothills of Ymir to seek the lost treasure. Your base is
a small bark hut that once belonged to a hermit.

Adapted from John Hardy's original Microbee introduction
(jhlagado/caverns, src/caverns-intro.basic, lines 22-38).

PLAYING ON TEC-OS

On the starter disk's main SYSTEM volume:
  CON PICO 80
  BAZA CAVERNS
  CAVERNS

Caverns is supplied as BASIC source. BAZA creates your executable.
TED CAVERNS.BAS opens it for reading or editing; Ctrl-S saves the file.
Lines are numbered in tens, leaving room to insert your experiments.
Keywords are UPPERCASE and variables lowercase.
To preserve the original, COPY CAVERNS.BAS CAVBOOK.BAS and edit that copy.
The compiled game includes its selected runtime. BAZA.RTL is needed to
compile, not to run. No UNLOAD is needed.

Find the lost treasures and bring them home. Returning through the hut door
with the key welcomes you home, deposits carried treasures, and displays
your score and ranking. Equipment is retained. Your possessions count double
at home. The maximum score is 126. You may leave again to explore, or QUIT.
After QUIT or death, N returns to TEC-OS and Y starts another adventure.
If you have a checkpoint, you can also type RESTORE at that prompt.

Commands use whole words, in either case:
  NORTH, SOUTH, EAST, WEST (or N/S/E/W)
  HELP, LOOK, LIST, INVENTORY, INVENT (or I), SCORE, QUIT
  SAVE, RESTORE (or LOAD)
  TAKE COMPASS, GET COMPASS, DROP COMPASS, PUT COMPASS
  ATTACK TROLL WITH SWORD (ATTACK TROLL SWORD also works)
  USE KEY, OPEN DOOR WITH KEY
  TIE ROPE TO RAIL, DOWN, UP, UNTIE ROPE
  OPEN HATCH
  LIGHT BOMB WITH CANDLE
HELP shows a short command list. HELP, LOOK, inventory, SCORE, SAVE and
RESTORE cost no turns. An incomplete attack asks for a weapon.

Take the compass before leaving. Without it you lose your bearings.
Room descriptions distinguish paths from distant views. Normal paths can
be retraced; locked passages explain what blocks them. A visible fatal
crater edge remains dangerous. Combat can still kill you, but fatigue clears
between encounters. A secured rope allows climbing both down and up.
Successful actions confirm what changed. Read the clues you discover.
The candle is currently found lit; matches are a later refinement.

SAVE holds one checkpoint in memory. Saving again replaces it. RESTORE
(or LOAD) brings back your room, inventory, creatures, puzzle progress,
candle and turn count. Combat rolls can differ after a restore. The slot
survives Y at the restart prompt. It lasts until you exit the program or
power off.

To keep source edits in the Viewer, save in TED and download the card image.
The card image preserves files. The in-game checkpoint lives only in memory.
